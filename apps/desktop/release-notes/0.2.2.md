This release fixes the reading of an account's trade history, which on any account with more than
a page of trades had never finished, and brings a reworked listing workspace, a live item page, and
the option of driving an engine running on another computer. The first start after updating
upgrades the trading record to schema 31, after copying it into `backups/` beside it. A build older
than 0.2.2 refuses to open the upgraded record and says so; to go back, reinstall the older build and
restore the copy.

**Trade history.** Every read of the trade history had asked Steam for the same page again and
again until Steam refused, so an account with more than a page of trades never had them on record,
and each attempt earned the next refusal. A read now takes a few older pages at a time, two minutes
apart and one page every fifteen seconds, and carries on from where it stopped rather than from the
top; the pages a refusal interrupts are kept. The account's page under *Settings* shows how much of
the market history and the trade history the record holds, and when a read is paused. A refusal
from Steam now holds that one read for ten minutes, and nothing asks again sooner, *Refresh now*
included; the rest of the account keeps reading.

**Listing workspace.** Staging items to sell opens one workspace for the selection. Each row shows
the Steam order book, bid, mid and ask, and *Price all rows* prices every row at once: sell now, top
bid, undercut, or the float curve's estimate. A price is sent in the account's own currency, typed in
it or pressed from a figure Steam named in it; a converted figure is only ever shown, marked ≈, and
never sent. An account whose wallet is in a currency the public book is not priced in gets its own
read of the book, signed in, so its prices can be pressed too.

**Items.** The item page's order book is live while the page is open, each read marked as it lands,
and sales volume is drawn behind the price line.

**Sales and deliveries.** A CSFloat sale whose accept or send was lost on the way is sent once more
after the next read proves it absent, and a venue's "try again later" is waited out for the time it
named. A sale Steam reversed inside its protection period reads as reversed on the Trades page, with
the item back under its own row and free to list again, and the bell says so. Requests to CSFloat
leave a tenth of every budget unspent, a fresh inventory is trusted for fifteen minutes, and several
sales waiting at once are accepted in one request.

**Untradable units.** An unsealed terminal is read as untradable, as Steam treats it, so it is never
offered for listing or transfer.

**Less text.** Page subtitles, empty-state hints and the "cached" badge are gone. What a figure
means is in a tip on the figure.

**Engine host.** *Settings › Engine host* connects this window to an engine running on another
computer, with that computer's Steam sessions. The host's pages, if it serves any, appear in the
sidebar. The token you type stays in the OS vault and is never shown back. The host itself is not
part of this release.

**For developers.** Every command records who submitted it, and a person's decision about an item
stands over an automated one until the person hands it back.
